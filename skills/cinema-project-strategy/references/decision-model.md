# Decision model

Use a Pareto comparison first: remove an option only if another is at least as suitable on every relevant dimension and better on one. Scores are aids to discussion, not measurements of greatness.

| Axis | Evidence to seek | What is not evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Artistic necessity | Specific human tension, author's stake, distinctive ending | Prestige adjectives, director name alone |
| Audience pull | Responses from intended viewers, retention on comparable format with denominator | Likes without reach, AI judges pretending to be viewers |
| Buyer fit | Real brief, expressed need, comparable delivered scope | Assumed brand interest |
| Feasibility | Accepted local shots, available assets, hardest-shot test | Model demo or untested tool list |
| Differentiation | Concrete contrast with closest comparables | Claim of universal originality |
| Learning/reuse | Reusable identity, environment, procedure or audience insight | Work already spent |

If scoring helps, use 0–4 with written anchors: 0 contradicts the brief; 1 weak/unsupported; 2 plausible with unresolved issue; 3 supported and strong; 4 unusually strong with direct evidence. Unknown is null, never zero. Choose weights for this brief and disclose them; vary major weights to see whether the winner changes. Keep dealbreakers outside the weighted sum.

Production cost = generation + labor hours × chosen rate + assets/licenses + post + delivery + revision allowance. Show cash cost and labor-inclusive cost separately. Successful selected seconds are the denominator; include discarded generations in the numerator. No accepted seconds means cost per accepted second is undefined.

For an economic scenario use net receipts minus all relevant costs. Probabilities require a stated basis; if none exists, show pessimistic/base/optimistic cases without labeling them expected value. A validation experiment specifies audience, artifact, question, sample size, observation and decision threshold before collecting responses. Small convenience samples guide iteration; they do not predict the whole market.
