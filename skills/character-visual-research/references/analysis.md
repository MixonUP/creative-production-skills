# Visual breakdown
For finalists, use concrete observations and separate observed facts from proposed reproduction.

1. Face architecture: skull and jaw silhouette, cheek planes, eye spacing/tilt, eyelid weight, nose abstraction, lip construction. Inspect front and three-quarter when available. Do not impose a universal face ratio.
2. Eye language: eye-to-face scale, iris value, sclera brightness, eyelid contact, gaze direction, catchlight. Large eyes alone do not guarantee warmth.
3. Hair: recognizable large masses, rhythm of clumps, face framing, flyaway density, highlight structure. Separate hairstyle identity from hair rendering.
4. Skin/materials: broad value transitions, selective microdetail, roughness contrast, subsurface impression, edge softness. Describe visible cues; do not infer the actual renderer from appearance.
5. Silhouette/costume: dominant shape, asymmetry, focal accent, recognizable accessory, material hierarchy. Charisma needs specificity beyond an attractive generic face.
6. Color/light: value grouping, warm/cool organization, key/fill separation, rim use, background contrast. Compare the face under neutral lighting to avoid selecting only cinematic grading.
7. Gesture/personality: pose tension, asymmetrical expression, habitual gaze, hands and posture. Write a short implied personality supported by the image, clearly as interpretation.
8. Detail hierarchy: where the eye rests; whether jewelry/noise competes with the face; what survives a small portrait.
9. Interaction: neutral, warm smile, amused, skeptical, vulnerable, focused; mouth opening and three-quarter turns. Check identity and emotional plausibility.
10. Differentiation: what distinguishes this heroine from the other cast members without relying only on hair color.

For each finalist deliver: source and provenance; 3–5 observable strengths; one limitation in the intended use; what to borrow (principle); what must become original (identity/costume); one testable scene.
“2.5D” may describe an appearance or a production system. Specify which. “Expensive-looking” is a curatorial impression unless a budget is documented.
