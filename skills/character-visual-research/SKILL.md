# Character visual research

Produce a reviewable visual decision, not an unsupported list of famous artists.

## Frame the search
Extract the user's taste, audience, character age, usage (portrait, dialogue, full-body, motion), references and requested breadth. Examples are search seeds, not boundaries. If the user can reproduce sophisticated styles with LoRA, do not eliminate ambitious art for presumed production difficulty. Separate exploration from later feasibility evaluation.

Read [evidence.md](references/evidence.md) when making claims about attractiveness, comfort or realism. Distinguish experimental findings, professional practice and curatorial judgment. There is no validated universal ranking of the most beautiful avatar style.

## Discover, then inspect
Search beyond the initial neighborhood: painted realism, cinematic CGI, sculptural stylization, graphic/anime, painterly animation, fashion/editorial and authored AI imagery. Follow the user's scope; these are optional search axes, not quotas.
Prefer artist portfolios and production credits; use aggregators to discover the original source. Distinguish studio assets, fan art, virtual photography, mod screenshots and AI art. Unknown provenance stays unknown.

For large requests, gather a broad candidate pool, then inspect actual pixels in manageable contact sheets and inspect shortlist images larger. A downloaded page is not a visual review. Count distinct works separately from files, crops, turntables and costume variants. Record rejected candidates and practical reasons; do not call all scraped items masterpieces.

Use [collection.md](references/collection.md) for acquisition and gallery handoff. Obtain public media at available resolution; never invent native 4K/8K from a filename or upscale. Keep author attribution and watermarks.

## Explain why each finalist works
Use [analysis.md](references/analysis.md). Describe observable features and their perceptual effect; avoid empty adjectives such as “premium, stunning, masterpiece.” Separate:
- intrinsic design: face, silhouette, expression, costume, personality;
- presentation: crop, lens/perspective, lighting, palette, finish;
- interaction: neutral face, smile, speech, gaze, emotional range.

Compare meaningful differences, including disagreements with the user's initial reference. A style family may have several strong artists without needing to imitate one whole portfolio.

## Make a choice testable
Provide a small finalist board alongside the full gallery. State a provisional recommendation as taste-informed judgment, not scientific fact.
Use [preference-tests.md](references/preference-tests.md) for controlled comparisons. Do not fabricate user scores or weights. Preserve favorites and rejection reasons. Keep visual quality, personal attraction, charisma and long-session comfort separate.

## Handoff
Deliver the gallery, manifest, research synthesis, shortlist, unresolved provenance/resolution limits, and a concrete art-direction recipe. For LoRA users, distinguish style from identity and recommend a held-out character comparison; use the relevant training skill only when dataset/config/training work is requested. Research does not authorize GPU jobs, paid subscriptions or publishing.
