# Cinematic voices and coherent scene sound

Use for native AI dialogue, designed/creature/radio voices, or a cinematic scene mix. First apply source-audio triage in `sound-finishing.md`; avoid unnecessary replacement of an effective native performance.

## Keep identity, performance and treatment separate

Voice card: character ID; authorized source/provider or native generation mode; accepted sample/take; stable register, timbre, pronunciation/accent and habitual delivery; pronunciation notes; version. Descriptive text alone is not a technical speaker lock. When identity drifts between shots, evaluate alternate takes, a supported identity/reference mechanism or a separately accepted voice route.

Scene performance: exact words, intention toward the listener, emotional and physical state, meaningful stresses, pauses, overlap and breaths. Exhaustion, whispering and shouting change delivery without automatically defining a new character. Align the performance with the dramatic beat and visible breath/mouth action.

Treatment: how the accepted voice exists in the scene (distance, room, occlusion, device, supernatural layer). Preserve the untreated source or original mixed source when no dry stem exists. Record treatment as a versioned recipe, not a vague request to make everything cinematic.

## Build an editable candidate treatment

The following is an authored engineering approach, not recovered HELL GRIND settings. Apply only the stages needed after listening:

1. Select and edit the performance. Identify clipping, intrusive noise, mouth artifacts and room discontinuities; repair conservatively and compare bypass. Avoid stripping intentional breaths or consonants.
2. Match adjacent dialogue through clip gain, appropriate tonal correction and controlled dynamics/de-essing when needed. A different voice cannot always be repaired into the same speaker with EQ. Do not hard-code compressor thresholds or frequency cuts without the actual signal.
3. Keep a clear intelligible voice path. For an intentional creature, radio or supernatural effect, test a separate parallel layer: pitch/formant, saturation, filtering or short modulation/delay as warranted. A pitch shift does not always preserve formants; label the algorithm and inspect intelligibility and artifacts. Avoid doubling identical near-aligned speech unintentionally.
4. Add spatial treatment appropriate to the camera and location: direct/room balance, early reflections/reverb, filtering/occlusion and position. An effect tail can cross a cut while the perspective changes. Keep identity treatment distinct from location treatment.
5. Automate competing ambience, impacts and music around essential words and reactions; preserve useful contrast and quiet. More bass, louder impacts and constant reverb do not by themselves make cinematic sound.

Recipe record: input and picture revisions; trim/time alignment; actual processor/version; stage order; parameter values; routing including parallel sends; automation; output specs; bypass/treated comparison and listening verdict. Unknown fields stay unknown. Enumerate installed processors and actual controls before implementation; do not invent a Fairlight MCP parameter API. Keep Resolve Free 21.0.3.7. Prepare an external-file handoff if needed and authorized; do not install another DAW automatically.

## Make sound a scene event

Use a continuous environment bed across related angles, with event layers for footsteps, objects, impacts and offscreen causes. Maintain scene-level cue IDs. For each decisive sound record cause, apparent location/distance, onset and tail, what the character hears, reaction timing and what the audience should infer. A deliberately offscreen sound needs no matching visible collision.

Preserve intelligible native speech/SFX when useful, supplement missing layers and watch for duplicate Foley. Source separation yields estimates, not original clean stems. Sound prompts and source cards describe intent; verify what is actually audible and what is retained in the final edit.

Review dry/original and treated candidates at comparable perceived loudness with the picture fixed. Listen for identity across at least the neighboring lines, intelligibility, breath/lip alignment, phase/comb effects in layered voices, room continuity, masking, transitions and effect tails. Measure peaks/loudness separately against the delivery requirement. Without actual listening, report preparation/technical checks only; user preference is evidence of taste, not an assistant's completed audio audit.
