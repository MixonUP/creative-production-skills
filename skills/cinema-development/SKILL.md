# Build the story before the spectacle

Start from the user's theme, audience, desired feeling and scale. Keep the author's values explicit. If these are missing, offer contrasting directions with clearly stated assumptions; do not hold all writing until a perfect brief arrives.

## Find a dramatic engine

Develop options that differ in human dilemma, point of view or causal structure. For each, identify who wants what, why now, what resists them, what the choice costs, and what image could embody the conflict. For lyrical or non-narrative work, define the organizing contrast and emotional transformation instead of forcing a hero's journey.

Test the premise without cinematic adjectives: does anything specific and consequential remain? An impossible image earns its place when it reveals character, changes the situation or expresses the theme. Use the pattern library in [story-workshop.md](references/story-workshop.md) only when it helps; the patterns are choices, not templates every film must obey.

## Develop at the requested depth

For this studio's linked analytics, writing packets and model comparison, read [studio-writing.md](references/studio-writing.md). Reuse an available decision packet without treating its estimated demand as fact or its choice as user approval.

Move from logline to treatment to causal beats and screenplay as needed. Write scenes in observable action and playable behavior. Separate narration, dialogue, silence and sound cues. Give a scene an entering state, an action/choice and a changed leaving state; a contemplative scene may change the viewer's understanding rather than external events.

For performance-driven scenes, read [scene-performance.md](references/scene-performance.md): turn intention, tactics and subtext into playable beats, character behavior and a clear scene-to-shot handoff. Keep the screenplay independent of generator prompt syntax; compile the relevant scene after its dramatic function is clear.

Check what the viewer knows at each reveal. Plant what the payoff needs. Replace explanatory dialogue with action when it increases clarity. Avoid treating an AI-generated script critique as audience research. Do not label a concept a masterpiece or predict awards.

## Keep production in the conversation

Flag expensive uncertainties: recurring identities, lip sync, hands/contact, crowds, long unbroken action, transformations and precise product details. Preserve the emotional function when proposing a cheaper staging; do not automatically reduce every ambitious idea to montage and voiceover. If dialogue or performance is essential, test it rather than hiding it.

Deliver the actual requested writing plus a short revision rationale and unresolved creative choices. For production handoff include scene IDs, intended duration ranges, cast/locations, emotional turns, essential visual/sound moments and difficult shots. Use `film-shot-planning` for the detailed shot contract and `cinema-visual-development` for continuity design.
