# Scene intention, performance and production handoff

Use this when dialogue, a reaction or a character choice carries the scene. Scale the record to the task; do not impose a fixed beat count on contemplative, comic or non-narrative work.

## Write the event before the prompt

For a scene, establish the entering situation, what each active character wants from someone/something now, the obstacle, cost of failure, and what new information or choice changes the leaving situation. Separate what the character knows from what the viewer knows. A shot exists to reveal, conceal, connect or make the viewer feel something specific.

A useful beat record is: trigger → tactic/action → observable response → changed knowledge/relationship. If the tactic fails, consider a new tactic rather than only louder delivery. Give subtext a playable consequence: a joke to deflect a question, an unfinished task, a held answer. Do not make every pause or gesture symbolic.

## Keep character behavior consistent without freezing it

Maintain a compact performance profile separate from costume and camera: habitual rhythm, physical task, selected behavior with its trigger, concealment strategy and the conditions that alter it. Adapt the profile to the actual posture and situation; a seated character need not inherit a walking instruction. Allow stillness, unusual behavior and intentional synchronized reactions when the scene calls for them. Do not require blinking, tics, a fixed distance or an emotional stereotype in every shot.

Separate stable vocal identity from scene delivery. A different emotion can alter breath, tempo and force without authorizing a different speaker identity. Reference the voice card; define exact spoken words, allowed overlaps, breaths and silences for this scene. Production chooses the actual native or external voice route.

For listening, specify the meaningful stimulus and a plausible response, including reaction timing when it matters. A sound can interrupt an ordinary physical task and create the turn. Make the response causally readable; do not automatically delay all reactions until dialogue finishes or force every reaction to begin early.

## Hand off scenes in editable blocks

Scene packet: scene ID and script revision; dramatic purpose; cast/state IDs; location/state; entering/leaving state; ordered beats; dialogue and sound cues; required reveals/reactions; continuity to adjacent scenes; risky actions. Shot planning decides coverage and camera, while preserving these functions.

Keep shared descriptors/versioned asset IDs outside repeated prose. Compile only active characters and props into the shot prompt; names in backstory or previous dialogue do not automatically become visible people. Define any previous audio context separately from words that must actually be spoken. Do not put a whole screenplay into every generation.

Original example: a technician wants to confirm a signal is real without alerting a partner. They dismiss it as interference while testing a switch. A reply repeats their private rhythm; the testing hand stops, then they hide the display. The event, task interruption and concealment support the scene. This is an illustrative structure, not a commissioned plot.
