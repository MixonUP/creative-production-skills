# Story workshop

## Useful engines

| Engine | What moves the story | Common failure | Revision lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moral choice | Two values cannot both be protected | Easy right answer | Give each path an actual cost |
| Mystery | Evidence changes an interpretation | Withholding arbitrary facts | Plant an interpretable clue before the reveal |
| Transformation | A coping strategy stops working | Change asserted in narration | Make the final action differ from the opening |
| Encounter | Two incompatible ways of living meet | One figure exists only to teach | Give each an independent need |
| Memory/essay | Recurrence changes a motif's meaning | Beautiful unrelated fragments | Return to an image with altered context |
| Commercial promise | A product enables a meaningful action | Generic spectacle with late logo | Make the product causal, without invented claims |

## Revision passes

Read for causality, then character, then image/sound, then production. Avoid rewriting all layers simultaneously. For each major note cite the scene or line, explain its effect and offer a concrete repair that preserves the film's intent.

Try the silent version: what is understood from action alone? Try the audio-only version: what emotional or spatial information does sound carry? These are diagnostic experiments, not requirements to remove dialogue or picture.

The final-image test asks how the ending changes the meaning of the beginning. The removal test asks what breaks if a scene is cut. Keep a quiet scene when its absence destroys rhythm, intimacy or comprehension.

For a short proof of concept, write one fully dramatized turn. Example original seed: a repairer who restores other people's recorded voices must choose whether to erase the last recording of a person they failed. The test is a visible, irreversible choice; the fictional technology and the character's behavior must support that choice. This is an illustrative seed, not a user-approved project.
