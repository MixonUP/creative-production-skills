# Review what the viewer actually receives

Establish the brief, film version, target screen/delivery, intended style and available media. State the inspection extent. Metadata establishes technical properties; contact sheets establish selected stills; only playback establishes motion and timing, and listening is needed for sound. If a modality is unavailable, leave its verdict unassessed.

## Review in layers

First view the sequence for comprehension and emotional progression without frame hunting. Note where attention, understanding or belief breaks. Then inspect problem intervals and transition boundaries. Use [review-rubric.md](references/review-rubric.md) for structured acceptance, not as a mandatory long report for a tiny correction.

Separate observed defects, interpretive creative notes and untested suggestions. Each important note names shot/timecode, evidence, viewer consequence, smallest repair and verification. Preserve deliberate stylization; unrealistic motion may be intentional. Compare against the work's own rules and brief.

## Prioritize repairs

Fix a wrong story action, broken identity or broken essential contact before enhancing detail. Prefer an edit, performance substitution or bounded composite when it preserves intent with less work than regeneration. Use `film-postproduction` for actual finishing; use `cinema-model-evaluation` when repeated defects suggest a route problem.

Do not call a film top 0.0001%, award-level or market-leading from an internal score. If audience validation is requested, test actual viewers and report question, sample, recruitment, exposure and limitations. Model-generated reactions are editorial hypotheses.

Deliver an acceptance verdict of accepted / accepted with named limitations / needs revision / not assessed, supported by inspected evidence. List only actionable priorities, distinguishing release blockers from optional polish. Record what was not inspected and avoid an overall pass when a required domain remains unknown.
